## Principle

Leverage comes from your best alternative to a negotiated agreement. Improve that, and
your position improves without saying anything.

## Explanation

The question that determines a negotiation is what happens to each side if no deal is
reached. Whoever has the better answer can accept less pressure, and both parties can
usually sense which of them that is.

This makes the decisive work preparatory rather than conversational. Techniques matter
at the margin; having a real alternative matters far more. An hour spent developing a
second option changes the outcome more than a day spent rehearsing arguments.

It also sets your floor. Any agreement worse than your alternative should be declined —
which is only possible if you have established what your alternative actually is,
rather than assuming you have none.

## Military Example

A negotiation over access to a route is shaped entirely by whether an alternative route
exists. Establishing a second option, even a slower and more expensive one, changes what
can be demanded — and the work of finding it happens well before anyone sits down.
Without it, the discussion is not a negotiation, it is a request.

## Leadership Example

Someone asking for a pay rise with no other offer and no willingness to leave is
requesting a favour. Their position is not improved by better arguments. It is improved
by having a genuine alternative — an offer, an internal move, a credible option — which
changes the conversation without ever needing to be mentioned. The same applies to the
employer, whose alternative is how easily the role can be refilled.

## Product Example

A team negotiating with a sole supplier of a critical API has no alternative, so terms
worsen at each renewal regardless of the relationship. The negotiation was decided
earlier, by the architecture. Building an abstraction layer and validating a second
provider costs engineering time and functions as leverage in every future discussion.

## Questions

- What do I do if there is no agreement, specifically?
- What is their alternative, and is it better or worse than mine?
- What could I do this week to improve my alternative?
- Am I about to accept something worse than walking away?

## Common Misuse

An overstated alternative is the standard failure — bluffing about options that do not
exist works until it is tested, and it usually gets tested. The concept also encourages
treating negotiations as purely adversarial, when most are repeated interactions where
the relationship outlasts the deal and a win extracted through leverage gets recovered
later. And a strong alternative is not a reason to use it: the goal is a good agreement,
not a demonstration of position.
