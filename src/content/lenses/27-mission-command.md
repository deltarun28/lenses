## Principle

Decisions belong at the level where the information is, not the level where the
authority traditionally sits.

## Explanation

Commander's Intent is about how you communicate purpose. Mission command is the wider
arrangement that makes acting on it possible: who is permitted to decide, what they can
commit, and what happens when their judgement differs from what you would have chosen.

The case for it is informational. The person nearest a problem sees things that never
survive the journey up a hierarchy, and by the time a decision returns, the situation
has moved. Centralised decision-making produces choices that were correct some hours
ago.

The cost is variation. Twelve people deciding independently will not decide
identically, and some will be wrong. Mission command is a trade: you accept a wider
spread of outcomes in exchange for speed and local accuracy. Organisations that want
the speed without the variation end up with neither, because the first reversed
decision teaches everyone to ask permission again.

## Military Example

A platoon commander who can commit their own reserve, alter a route and coordinate
directly with a flanking unit acts inside the time an adversary needs to respond. The
same commander required to seek approval for each is slower than the situation. The
enabling conditions are not motivational: they are stated intent, understood
constraints, and a superior who does not punish a reasonable decision that turned out
badly.

## Leadership Example

A support organisation authorises any agent to issue a refund below a set value without
approval. Resolution times fall sharply and a small number of refunds are ones a manager
would have declined. The saving is real and the leakage is real, and the decision is
whether the second is worth the first. Reversing individual refunds publicly would
recover a little money and destroy the whole arrangement.

## Product Example

A team empowered to decide its own implementation approach within an agreed outcome
ships faster than one whose technical choices are approved centrally. Some choices will
be worse than the architect would have made. The comparison is not against the ideal
decision; it is against the same decision made three weeks later by someone who has not
read the code.

## Questions

- Who has the information this decision actually depends on?
- What can this person commit without asking, and do they know it?
- When someone made a reasonable call that went badly, what happened to them?
- Am I willing to accept the variation, or do I want the speed without the cost?

## Common Misuse

Mission command gets announced without changing what people are permitted to do, which
produces the worst of both: apparent autonomy, real punishment for using it. It also
does not apply universally — irreversible commitments, safety-critical work and legally
constrained decisions belong at the level accountable for them. And it presumes
competence and shared understanding; delegated to someone lacking either, it is not
empowerment, it is abandonment.
