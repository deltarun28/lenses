## Principle

You can only study what survived. What it took to survive is invisible in that sample.

## Explanation

Success is studied because success is available. The failures did not write books, give
interviews or stay in the dataset, so any pattern found among survivors may be common
to the whole population — including everyone it did not save.

The classic case is aircraft returning from operations with damage concentrated in
certain areas. The temptation is to armour where the damage is. The correct inference is
the reverse: those are the places an aircraft can be hit and still return. The evidence
about the fatal areas is missing precisely because it did not come back.

The correction is to ask, every time, what the sample excludes and whether the excluded
cases shared the trait you are about to adopt.

## Military Example

After-action reviews are conducted with the people and units that came through. Their
accounts describe what worked for them. The techniques used by those who did not return
are absent from the record, and so are the cases where the same technique failed. This
is why loss investigations are conducted separately and why absence of a report is not
evidence of absence.

## Leadership Example

An organisation studies its highest performers to identify the traits to hire for, and
finds they are unusually willing to override process. Promoting that trait assumes the
overriders who caused damage are visible in the sample. They are not — they left, or
never got promoted, and their absence is the entire reason the trait looks good.

## Product Example

Founder advice reliably describes persistence through a period when everyone said the
idea was wrong. The advice is sincere and the sample is fatal: the far larger group who
persisted through the same signals and failed did not get asked. "Ignore the evidence
and keep going" is a rule that appears in both populations, and only one of them is
publishing.

## Questions

- What is missing from this sample, and why is it missing?
- Did the failures share the trait I am attributing to the successes?
- Am I studying the outcome or the process that produced it?
- Where would I find the cases that did not make it?

## Common Misuse

The concept becomes a way to dismiss any evidence from successful examples, which
overcorrects — sometimes survivors really do share a causal advantage, and the fact
that a sample is selected does not make it uninformative. It is also invoked
selectively, usually against conclusions the speaker dislikes. The productive move is
to go and find the missing cases, not to use their absence as a general-purpose
objection.
