## Principle

Identify what an opponent's capability actually depends on, and direct effort there.

## Explanation

Every capability rests on something: a resource, a relationship, a belief, a supply
chain, a single scarce competence. That foundation is the centre of gravity, and effort
directed at it produces disproportionate effect. Effort directed at the visible outputs
produces attrition.

The analysis works backwards. What must remain true for this capability to persist? Keep
asking until you reach something that is both essential and reachable. Essential but
unreachable is interesting and useless; reachable but inessential is where most effort
is wasted.

The same analysis applied to yourself is more uncomfortable and more valuable, because
your own centre of gravity is the thing you should be protecting rather than optimising.

## Military Example

An adversary's ability to sustain operations may rest on a small number of bridging
assets, a single fuel distribution node, or the cohesion of a coalition. Destroying
fielded units addresses the output. Interdicting the node addresses the source, and the
units become ineffective without being engaged. Identifying which it is, rather than
attacking what is visible, is the whole of the analysis.

## Leadership Example

A rival department's influence rests not on its budget but on one relationship with the
executive and its control of a reporting line everyone else depends on. Competing on
budget is attrition. Establishing an independent source for that reporting changes the
position without a confrontation, because the influence was never really about the
money.

## Product Example

A competitor's advantage looks like their feature set, which invites a feature race that
they win because they got there first. Examined properly, the advantage rests on
proprietary data accumulated through a distribution channel. Matching features leaves
that untouched. Building a different route to comparable data attacks the actual source,
and takes longer than any feature.

## Questions

- What must remain true for this capability to keep working?
- Am I acting on the source of their strength or on its visible output?
- Is what I have identified both essential and reachable?
- What is my own centre of gravity, and how exposed is it?

## Common Misuse

The term gets applied loosely to anything important, at which point it stops
discriminating — if a plan lists six centres of gravity, none of them is one. It also
encourages single-point thinking about systems that are genuinely redundant, where the
apparent linchpin has three substitutes and removing it achieves nothing. And the
analysis is only as good as the intelligence behind it: acting decisively against a
wrongly identified centre is worse than attrition, because it commits everything to the
wrong place.
