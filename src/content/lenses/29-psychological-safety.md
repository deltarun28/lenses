## Principle

A team's real performance depends on whether people can say the inconvenient thing
without cost.

## Explanation

The information a leader most needs — this will not work, I made a mistake, the number
is wrong — is exactly the information that is personally risky to deliver. Where that
risk is high, it does not get delivered, and the leader operates on a picture that has
been quietly cleaned.

Psychological safety is not comfort, agreeableness or the absence of disagreement. Teams
with high safety argue more, not less. What is absent is the fear that raising a problem
will be treated as being a problem.

It is established by what happens after someone speaks, not by inviting them to. One
public cost imposed on a person who reported bad news teaches everyone watching, and it
takes a long time to undo.

## Military Example

Aircrew and maintenance cultures depend on near-miss reporting: a fault reported early
is a maintenance task, and the same fault unreported is a loss. Reporting systems are
therefore built with explicit protection, because a technician who expects blame will
resolve an ambiguous observation as "probably fine" — not from dishonesty, but because
that is the rational response to the risk.

## Leadership Example

A leader responds sharply to a project status that has slipped to red. What is learned
is not that slipping is unacceptable — that was already known — but that reporting a
slip is. The next project reports green until the week it is due. The information the
leader needed most arrived last.

## Product Example

An engineer notices a design flaw in a feature already announced. Whether they raise it
depends entirely on what happened the last time someone questioned a committed decision.
The cost of hearing it now is an awkward conversation; the cost of hearing it after
launch is the rework plus the customer damage, and the team knew.

## Questions

- When did someone last tell me something I did not want to hear, and what happened to them?
- Is the absence of bad news evidence that there is none?
- Do people disagree with me in front of others, or only afterwards?
- What does my reaction to a mistake teach everyone who witnesses it?

## Common Misuse

The term gets stretched to mean freedom from discomfort or from accountability, which
inverts it — safety to report a problem is not immunity from performance standards, and
teams that conflate the two become unable to address anything. It is also treated as a
survey score to manage rather than a condition to produce. And it cannot be declared:
announcing an open door changes nothing about what happens when someone walks through
it.
