## Principle

Communicate why and what success looks like, so that subordinates can decide correctly
when circumstances change.

## Explanation

Detailed instructions are only valid for the situation they were written for. As soon as
conditions change, someone must decide whether to follow instructions that no longer
fit — and without knowing the purpose, they can only guess.

Intent solves this by stating three things: the purpose, the desired end state, and the
constraints that must not be violated. Given those, someone on the ground can construct
a new plan that still serves the goal.

It is harder than issuing instructions, because it requires knowing your own purpose
precisely. Vague intent is worse than none: it produces confident divergence, with
everyone acting on a different reading of what you meant.

## Military Example

A company is told to secure a bridge. Told only that, they hold it as ordered even after
the crossing is no longer needed. Told the purpose — to allow the brigade to cross by
first light — and the constraint — the bridge must remain intact — they can adapt when
the brigade's route changes, and can recognise that a different crossing point now
serves the same intent.

## Leadership Example

A team is told to reduce support response times. They meet the target by closing tickets
faster, which increases repeat contacts. Told the intent — customers should be able to
resolve problems without a second contact, without adding headcount — they would have
made different trade-offs, some of which would have looked worse against the original
instruction.

## Product Example

An engineering team asked to build a specified admin dashboard builds exactly that.
Given the intent — support staff should be able to diagnose an account problem without
escalating to engineering — they discover that three of the requested charts are
useless and one missing log view solves most cases. The specification was a guess at how
to achieve the purpose, and only the purpose was worth protecting.

## Questions

- If circumstances change tomorrow, does my team know enough to decide without me?
- Have I stated the purpose and the end state, or only the task?
- What are the genuine constraints, as opposed to my preferences about method?
- Could someone restate my intent in their own words and get it right?

## Common Misuse

Intent gets used as cover for vagueness — an unclear goal delegated is not empowerment,
it is abdication, and the resulting divergence gets blamed on the people who acted.
It also does not apply everywhere: safety-critical procedures and regulated work need
specified method, not adaptation. And intent only works when the person receiving it has
the competence and information to act on it; issued to someone who lacks both, it
produces confident errors.
