## Principle

If replacing the people would not change the outcome, the problem is the system.

## Explanation

Persistent problems are rarely explained by the individuals involved. When a pattern
survives multiple changes of personnel, it is being produced by structure: how work
flows, what gets measured, who can decide what, and where delays sit between cause and
effect.

Systems thinking asks what arrangement makes the observed behaviour the sensible
response. People generally act rationally given their constraints and information, so
persistent irrational-looking behaviour is a sign that the constraints are doing the
work.

The practical consequence is where you intervene. Exhortation, training and new
personnel change behaviour temporarily. Changing flow, measurement, or decision rights
changes it durably.

## Military Example

Maintenance backlogs persist across three successive squadron commanders. The cause is
structural: spares are held centrally, requisitions are approved weekly, and the
approval authority sits two levels above the person who knows the aircraft state. Each
commander improves things briefly through personal energy, then the pattern returns. The
durable fix is moving the approval authority down, not appointing a fourth commander.

## Leadership Example

An organisation complains that departments do not collaborate. The structure explains
it: budgets are allocated per department, performance is assessed per department, and
any shared initiative costs one department resources it is measured on. Collaboration
is not a values problem here. It is a design that makes cooperation individually
expensive, and no amount of cultural messaging will outweigh that.

## Product Example

Quality problems recur despite hiring strong engineers. The structure is the cause:
testing sits with a separate team, release dates are fixed before scope is known, and
the cost of a defect falls on support rather than on the team that shipped it. Every
individual decision is reasonable. The output is unreliable software, and it will stay
that way until the feedback reaches the people making the decisions.

## Questions

- Would this behaviour persist if I replaced everyone involved?
- What makes the current behaviour the rational choice for the people doing it?
- Where does the delay sit between an action and its consequence?
- Who feels the cost of this problem, and are they the ones who can fix it?

## Common Misuse

Systems language becomes a way to avoid accountability: if everything is the system,
nobody is responsible, and genuinely poor performance goes unaddressed. Individuals do
matter, particularly at the top, where they set the structure. The other failure is
analysis without intervention — elaborate diagrams of feedback loops that change
nothing. The purpose is to find the small number of places where a change in structure
alters the pattern.
