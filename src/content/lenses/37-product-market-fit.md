## Principle

Fit is when the market pulls the product out of you. Before that, effort spent scaling
is wasted.

## Explanation

The distinguishing symptom is directional. Without fit, everything requires pushing:
each customer is individually persuaded, usage decays after onboarding, and growth
stops the moment attention stops. With it, people arrive without being chased, use it
without prompting, and complain when it breaks.

The practical value of the concept is sequencing. Almost everything an organisation
wants to do — hire a sales team, build a brand, optimise a funnel — multiplies whatever
is already happening. Applied before fit, they multiply nothing at considerable expense.

The reliable evidence is behavioural: do people come back unprompted, and does a
meaningful group say they would be genuinely disrupted if it disappeared? Enthusiasm in
interviews is not evidence, because enthusiasm is free.

## Leadership Example

An executive under pressure for growth hires a sales team before the product retains
customers. The team performs: acquisition rises, churn rises with it, and the cost of
each customer exceeds what they return. The failure is read as a sales problem and
answered with more salespeople. Nothing about the hire was wrong except its position in
the sequence.

## Product Example

A team sees healthy sign-up numbers and month-four retention near zero. That is not a
funnel to be optimised; it is an absence of fit, and every improvement to the funnel
adds people to a bucket with a hole in it. The useful work is with the small number who
did stay: what were they doing that the others were not, and can the product be narrowed
to that?

## Questions

- Are people arriving and returning without being pushed?
- Would a meaningful number be genuinely disrupted if this vanished?
- Am I about to multiply something that is not yet working?
- Which small group is already getting real value, and what do they have in common?

## Common Misuse

The term is used as a binary achievement — announced, then assumed permanent. It is
neither binary nor stable: fit exists for a specific segment at a specific time, and it
is lost when the market moves. It is also used to defer every uncomfortable question
indefinitely, since "we don't have fit yet" excuses the absence of a plan. And the
signals are easy to fake to yourself, because early enthusiasm from people who like you
looks exactly like demand.
